# Tiro landing reference

Source: https://tiro.ooo/ko/

Note: Cursor Cloud instructions prohibit Computer Use and interactive screenshot loops. This analysis uses read-only page extraction and translates the observed section/content structure into landing decisions.

## Section sequence and intent

1. Hero: leads with a concrete job-to-be-done, "focus on conversation" plus a claim of accuracy.
2. Social proof: repeats audience segments and shows named testimonials early.
3. Feature proof: records, summarizes, translates, and formats meetings with concrete speed/quality bullets.
4. Device/platform coverage: answers usage objections across web, desktop, mobile, online/offline.
5. Workflow expansion: turns meetings into templates, sharing, integrations, and AI Q&A.
6. Security: deep enterprise trust section with certification, encryption, access control, deletion, audit logs.
7. Pricing and FAQ: monetization and objection handling at the end.

## Content strategy

- The page makes the product feel practical by translating AI into repeated daily outcomes.
- The strongest content pattern is "user anxiety → product promise → operational details."
- Trust is not a small badge; it is a full section with many security primitives.

## Design style

- Friendly Korean SaaS copy with large direct claims and many short benefit blocks.
- Long scroll, section-by-section reassurance, heavy testimonial density.
- Uses feature blocks to turn an abstract AI product into concrete moments.

## What SSOTA should adapt

- Lead with the human pain: "agents move fast, but intent drifts faster."
- Add named workflow/persona blocks for founder, PM, developer, agency.
- Include a serious trust/control section: approval, audit trail, decision history, MCP write-back.
- Use concrete bullets inside the product preview, not vague platform language.

## What SSOTA should avoid

- Do not over-index on generic "AI productivity" claims.
- Avoid a pricing-heavy page for this first landing; the Primer story needs wedge clarity first.
